# 3.7.1

- A link to the cancel route left in an overridden payment template (`path('payzen_embedded_front_abort_payment', ...)` in an `<a href>`, as in 3.6.0 and before) now opens a confirmation page instead of a 405: "Cancel the payment of order X?", with the POST form of 3.7.0 and a link back to the payment step. # 3.7.0

# 3.6.0

Requires Thelia 3.3.0.

- Payments split between several means: when instant means (a gift card, a credit note) paid part of an order, PayZen charges the balance of its own part, not the total of the order. The module implements `BalanceChargingPaymentModuleInterface`: the amount sent to PayZen is `getAmountToCharge()`, which is the total of the order when nothing else paid a part of it. The module names itself as the source of every status change it asks for, so that the core settles the part PayZen holds when PayZen confirms the payment, and keeps for a refund a payment PayZen confirms on an order the shop cancelled meanwhile.
- The cards PayZen registers for the 1-click payment are payment methods of the shop (`PaymentModuleWithStoredMethodsInterface`): they are kept with the core, with a label such as "Visa •••• 4242" and their expiry, listed at the checkout, and managed in the customer account. A customer pays with one of them by choosing it; choosing a new card never sends a stored one (the card used to be attached to every payment of its customer). Forgetting a card cancels the token at PayZen first (`Token/Cancel`): a card PayZen did not cancel stays kept, and the customer gets a message saying so. Whatever fails on the module side while forgetting a card is a refusal too, never a server error. The token is in no log, no message and no answer of the module.
- A card is tied to the PayZen contract it was registered on: PayZen creates the token on the contract of the sale unit of the paid order (3.5.0), and the token is only good there. A new table of the module, `payzen_embedded_stored_method_contract` (`customer_payment_method_id` primary key, foreign key cascading on `customer_payment_method`, `sale_unit_id` null for the national contract, no foreign key on the sale unit so that deleting one neither erases the link nor is refused for it), is written when the card is registered (the sale unit of the order; a card already kept keeps its link) and read for everything else. The checkout lists a card only for the sale unit of the cart (a listener of the module runs after the core's one on the payment options event and drops the cards of other sale units, and the group altogether when none is left), `payWithStoredMethod()` and `CardFormProvider::forOrder()` refuse a card of another sale unit (`StoredMethodOfAnotherContractException`, a refusal of the core's `CustomerPaymentMethodException` family, answered 422), and forgetting a card cancels it on the contract of its own sale unit, whatever sale unit the request resolved, the anonymization and the deletion of the customer included: they drop the card, and its link with it, before PayZen is asked, so the module reads the link before the drop (`DeferredStoredMethodForgettingPaymentModuleInterface`). A national card pays national orders only: a sale unit that leaves its contract empty reads the national one, but may set any setting of a contract, and the module does not compare contracts. A card with no link (a failed write) is of an unknown contract: it pays nowhere and is cancelled on the national contract. Create the table by `Config/create.sql` (installation) and `Config/update/3.6.0.sql` (replayable, no DDL when the table exists, which would commit the transaction of a caller). The update ties every card of the module, the ones migrated included, to the national contract: the old table did not keep the sale unit, so a card registered in 3.5.0 on the own contract of a sale unit is tied to the wrong contract and fails when paid with (see the Readme, "One card, one contract").
- The card form opens on the card chosen on the cart: `CardFormProvider::forOrder($order)` without a card resolves the one the core resolves for the payment (`StoredPaymentMethodPayment::methodChosenFor()`), whoever asks (the module's `pay()` or a theme that mounts the fields in its own step), and opens a new card form when none is chosen, or when the card is of another sale unit. The platform is asked once per order and card in a request, whichever of `pay()` and a theme asks first (the key is the card the form opens on, not the way it was named). The hourly allowance of payments with a stored card stays spent by the core in `StoredPaymentMethodPayment::pay()`, once per payment: opening a form spends nothing, and a form not yet opened on a resolved card is opened on a new card once the customer has spent the allowance (read, never spent), so that the cap is not bypassed.
- The checkout notice (`one_click-token-info.html`) no longer promises a payment made at once on "Next Step": the form opens on the saved card, the customer confirms the payment there and the bank may ask for a 3-D Secure authentication. The notice is shown for a customer who has a card that can pay on the sale unit of the request. (Translations changed: the keys "You can pay with a saved card." and "Your credit card details have been saved at your request. If you pay with a saved card, ...".)
- A card is registered from the notification or the answer of a payment only (never from a refresh of a transaction, an update or a capture), and only for a payment PayZen took or is taking: a refused attempt and a refund register nothing. A failure to keep the card no longer makes the notification fail.
- Update script `3.6.0.sql`: the tokens of `payzen_embedded_customer_token` are moved into `customer_payment_method`, with a generic label, and the oldest card becomes the default of a customer who had none. A guest and an anonymized account are not migrated, and their rows are kept by the script: PayZen still holds their cards, and a row is all that is left to cancel them with. An account that no longer exists and an empty token are not migrated and their rows are deleted; so is every row once its card is kept, so that a card the customer forgot is never brought back by a replay. Replayable. The table is left in place for this version and is no longer read.
- After the update, cancel at PayZen the cards of guests and anonymized accounts left in the old table: `php Thelia payzen-embedded:legacy-tokens:cancel` (`--limit`, 50 by default; `--after-id` to start after a row PayZen keeps refusing, which the command's output suggests). The update itself calls PayZen for none of them (it runs while the module is deactivated, and a call may wait for its timeout): it only writes a warning in the log with their number. The command asks PayZen with short timeouts (5 s to connect, 10 s for the answer), cancels at most `--limit` cards per run, drops the row of each card PayZen cancelled and keeps the others, and prints the cancelled and failed counts, `Remaining` (every row still in the table, the cards PayZen refused included: it never reaches 0 while PayZen refuses a card), the last row tried and the rows left after it; it never prints or logs a token. When a card failed it prints the command to run next, with `--after-id <last row>`. Replayable: run it again, or schedule it, advancing with `--after-id`, until no row is left after the last one tried; the cards that stay are the ones PayZen refuses, to be looked at in the PayZen back office.
- A `Token/Cancel` refused with `PSP_1047` ("Alias already cancelled") is a success at once, without a read. Any other refusal is read again with `Token/Get`: a token the platform reports as already cancelled (a cancellation date) is gone, which is what forgetting a card asked for, so the cancellation succeeds and the shop drops the card. Any other case (the token is still active, unknown or invalid, `PSP_1048`, `PSP_030`, `PSP_1049`, `PSP_031` and `PSP_558` included, the read is refused or fails) stays a refusal, and the card stays kept: an alias unknown on this contract may exist on another one. The same rule applies to `payzen-embedded:legacy-tokens:cancel`.
- Known limit, one card, one contract: a card registered before 3.6.0 on the own contract of a sale unit is tied to the national contract by the update (the old table did not keep the sale unit), and PayZen does not know its token there. It cannot pay, and the customer has to enter the card again. The stored token stays at PayZen until it is cancelled from the PayZen back office.
- Changed signatures of the actions of `PayzenEmbedded\Controller\FrontController`, whose arguments are injected: `processIpn(EventDispatcherInterface $dispatcher, Translator $translator)` becomes `processIpn(EventDispatcherInterface $dispatcher, Translator $translator, StoredMethodRecorder $storedMethodRecorder)`, and `clearCustomerToken(Session $session)` becomes `clearCustomerToken(Session $session, CustomerPaymentMethodService $storedMethods, TokenProvider $tokenProvider, Translator $translator)`. A module or a theme that extends the controller and overrides either method has to follow.
- Known limit: a customer deleted before the update takes their row with them (the foreign key of the old table cascades), and the token stays active at PayZen, out of the shop's reach. Cancel it from the PayZen back office if needed.
- The `payzen_embedded_token` loop and the account block read the payment methods of the shop. `/payzen-embedded/alias-clear` forgets them and now answers a POST that carries the CSRF token of the session (the account block posts a form; the checkout block links to the account page, as the hook is rendered inside the payment form of the theme): a link can no longer cancel a customer's cards, and a visitor gets a 403. Forgetting the cards stops when PayZen cannot be reached (the next card would wait for the same timeout); a card PayZen refuses on its own is kept and the next one is asked. The customer is told how many cards remain, the refused ones and the ones not tried included.
- A payment PayZen confirms on an order the shop refuses to pay (cancelled meanwhile, its other parts given back) is no longer a server error: the history row and the transaction reference are kept so that the payment can be refunded from the order, an error naming the order is logged (`paid after cancellation, to refund`), and the notification is answered, so PayZen stops replaying it. A transient failure still answers a server error.
- The answer `200` to a payment confirmed on an order the shop refuses to pay is given only when the order, read again, is cancelled, already paid (paid, processing, sent) or refunded; the log says "after cancellation" for a cancelled order and "again on an order already paid/refunded" for a second payment. A refusal of the transition graph of the shop on any other order stays a server error, so that the platform replays the notification.
- Lowering the amount of a transaction is bounded by what PayZen holds for the transaction (its recorded amount, or the part of the order PayZen holds), not by the total of an order paid in several parts.

# 3.4.0

## Cancel or refund from the order page
- A payment can be cancelled or refunded from the order page of the back-office, in full or in part (`Transaction/CancelOrRefund`, `resolutionMode AUTO`). The platform decides: a transaction waiting for its capture is cancelled, in full, whatever its validation mode; a captured one gets a refund transaction, as many times as needed up to the amount paid. The order moves to the refunded status once the platform confirmed every refund and nothing is left to refund, to the cancelled status when the transaction was cancelled, and stays as it is after a partial or a pending refund.
- `PayzenEmbedded::TRANSACTION_REFUND_EVENT` does the same from code, for instance from a returns or picking module: dispatch a `TransactionRefundEvent` with the order, the amount in minor units, a reason, the administrator and, optionally, the amount seen as refunded so far, then read `getOutcome()` (`Cancelled`, `Refunded`, `PartiallyRefunded` or `Pending`).
- Refunding needs the permission on the orders as well as on the module, and so do the transaction update and the history refresh, which needed the permission on the module only: a profile that captured or refreshed without the permission to update orders has to be granted it; the order page shows the transaction forms to the administrators allowed to use them. Every attempt, failed or not, is written to the administrator log.

## Guards on the refund
- The capture after picking never asks for more than the platform holds for the order: an authorisation taken before this version truncated a legacy total to the cent below, where the total is now rounded, and a capture one cent above it would have been refused. A capture cut by more than a cent is logged as an error.
- The answer to a transaction update moves the order first and is recorded afterwards, so that an authorisation captured moves the order to paid as before: a host module that goes on from the paid status (a pickup notification) still does.
- A refund whose answer never came (a timeout, or an answer that could not be recorded) holds the order: no other refund is asked for until the platform lists as much refunded as that request would have made, nothing is left to give back, or ten minutes have passed, since the platform's list may not show it yet and the same money would go twice.
- Before the balance is checked, the platform's own list of the order's transactions is recorded (`Order/Get`): a refund whose answer was lost, or one made from the PayZen back-office, is counted. A refund never moves the order: when the platform holds the transaction the order stands on in another state than the history (cancelled, or captured), the refund is refused and asks for the history refresh, which moves the order as the notification would; when the refresh cannot bring the order along (a status change refused for want of stock, say), the message points to the PayZen back-office. The balance is read on the transaction the order stands on, so the attempts the shopper gave up on do not add up; a refund still running, or in a state the module does not know, counts as money already promised, never offered twice, but not as money given back until the platform confirms it.
- The refund form carries what the page showed as refunded so far: a refund the page did not show is refused until the page is reloaded, since asking again would refund twice. One refund at a time per order, under the framework's lock (see the Readme for a shop served by several servers): the history refresh refuses a locked order, the transaction update (the capture after picking) waits for it up to 15 seconds, and the form button is disabled once the refund is confirmed. The figure shown as refunded so far is required by the form; a `TransactionRefundEvent` dispatched without it relies on the lock and on the balance alone. The amount is typed in, never prefilled nor locked, even for a cancellation: the history may be behind the platform (a payment captured since), and the page then says to refresh it.
- The platform's answer is read before anything is written: only a credit, or the debit back as cancelled, is accepted. A credit the platform refused is refused and the order is left alone; any other answer the module cannot read holds the order as a lost answer does, since the money may have moved. A request the platform did not answer, or an answer that could not be recorded, is reported as such, never as "not sent", and the next attempt starts by reading the platform. A refund nothing handled (a listener stopped the event) is reported as a failure.
- The update form compares its amount to the order total in the smallest unit of the currency: a legacy total with four decimals refused the amount the form was filled with. Its guidance says the amount can only be lowered, as the rule does.
- Amounts typed by an administrator are read strictly into the smallest unit of the currency ("1 234,56" or "12abc" are refused; "4,50" was sent as 4.00 by the update form), with the decimals of the currency (none for JPY or XPF, three for KWD or TND). A total the shop computes, such as the capture after picking, is converted whatever its decimals.

## Transaction history and notifications
- A credit the module asks for is tied to the debit it asks it on when the platform's answer does not name it, and a transaction the history already holds for the order is recognised as the shop's when the platform lists or notifies it again: a refund still running is seen settled once confirmed. An answer that does not repeat a credit's parent no longer erases it, and a credit is counted positive whatever the sign it is written with.
- An order another payment of which the platform took (a double payment) is not set refunded while that payment is held, and its page says so. The order is read again under the lock before the operation decides, the answer to a transaction update is recorded in the history, a failure of the lock store shows a fixed message, and the history keeps the order of rows written in the same second. A signed notification the module fails on is answered with a server error, so that the platform replays it and warns the merchant; when the order could not move (a status change refused for want of stock, say), what the notification wrote in the history and the order reference is undone, so that the replay, or the history refresh, applies it again instead of finding it applied. The order is read under a row lock: nothing is undone once the order was saved after its reference was set (a host module that brings it back to its own status has moved it all the same), nor over what another notification or a refresh wrote meanwhile. The history rows are read again after each call to the platform.
- An answer that does not say a transaction's type no longer turns a credit the history holds into a debit, and a transaction marked by another shop stays out even when the history knows it. The trace of a refund answer keeps its shape (parent, shop marker) without the card data the transaction details carry.
- The same transaction notified or read again in the same state (a refresh of an authorisation still running) no longer moves back an order that left the unpaid state: an order being prepared stayed prepared, where it went back to paid and the confirmation was sent again.
- The creation dates of the history are read back as the UTC the platform gave them: the column keeps no time zone and a shop outside UTC read its own history hours off the notifications it weighed, which could let the late refusal of an earlier attempt cancel an order standing on a later one. The transactions of one notification are judged debits first, each on the order the previous one left: a credit sent with its debit is no longer set aside. The refresh names, in a warning, an attempt the platform lists that outranks the one the order stands on, since only its notification moves the order.
- The notification endpoint accepts only an answer signed with the REST password, as the platform signs its notifications. The answer the shopper's browser receives, signed with the public HMAC key, was accepted there too and could be replayed by the shopper.
- A payment the platform took always outranks an unpaid transaction the order stands on, whatever the order of the attempts: a refusal of a later attempt notified first no longer leaves the payment of an earlier one on a cancelled order. A payment notified once every cent of it was already given back (its refund notified or listed first) settles the order as refunded instead of paid.
- An order that carries no transaction reference was never moved by a notification: the transactions the refresh records for it are the platform's word, not a move. It takes any payment, a refusal is weighed against a payment the platform lists only, it is never set refunded, and its page offers neither a refund nor an update until a notification ties it to its payment.
- A new `operationType` column tells a refund (`CREDIT`) from a payment (`DEBIT`), `parentUuid` names the debit a credit gives money back on. Refunds show in the history with a negative amount. A shop reading the history by status has to keep the debits: a credit is `PAID` too (`payzen_embedded_history` takes `operation_type="DEBIT"`). The rows written before this version are marked as debits by the update script. `detailedStatus` is widened to 64 characters and `status` to 32: the platform's `WAITING_AUTHORISATION_TO_VALIDATE` did not fit in 32, nor `PARTIALLY_PAID` in 10. Depending on the SQL mode of the server, a value too long was cut, then read back as another status, or refused with the row and the refund. Every value the platform sends is now also cut to its column before it is written.
- A credit given back that the platform refuses afterwards is logged as an error: the order status is left to the shop.
- A credit the platform finished (given back or refused) is never put back as running by a notification that arrives late or is replayed.
- A credit notified by the platform is recorded and moves the order only to the refunded status, once every refund of it is confirmed and nothing is left to refund, whoever asked for the refund. A notification for an order paid with another module is ignored. The platform is answered about the transaction the order stands on, whatever the order of the transactions in its notification.
- Every payment carries a shop marker in its metadata, and what the platform lists or notifies is checked against it, with the space (TEST or PRODUCTION): two shops on one contract, or two environments in the TEST space, produce the same order references. A transaction without a marker is tied to an order only when it is the debit the order stands on, or a credit of that debit; a payment notified without one is still accepted, since a payment precedes the marker. See the Readme before changing the shop URL.

## Twig back-office fixes
- The PayZen block reads the order id whichever name the back-office hands it: the Twig order page hands `order` to `order-edit.bottom` where every other hook receives `order_id`.
- The "Refresh history" button is offered on every order paid with PayZen, with or without a history, and also records every transaction the platform lists for the order (`Order/Get`): a refund or a cancellation made from the PayZen back-office now shows in the history, where the notification arbiter alone left a finished transaction untouched. A refund confirmed by the platform that leaves nothing to refund moves the order to the refunded status, as its notification does. A cancellation cancels the order while the history holds the authorisation as running, as its notification does; one of a payment the history already holds as paid leaves the order status to the shop. The refresh records the platform's list first, then reads the transaction the order stands on: a payment refunded in full on the platform takes the order to refunded without passing through paid (and a host's notice on paid). The transaction the list held back is then written as listed, unless something wrote it since the list was read. The refresh holds the same lock as the refund, so the two never write the history at the same time. An order that still carries no transaction (its payment was never notified) is refreshed with a warning: the history is recorded, but only the notification ties the order to its transaction.
- The transaction update reports a failure when nothing handled its event or the platform answered an unexpected status, instead of "updated".
- The order page of a transaction still open broke on the capture date widget; the outcome and the errors of the three actions went to the parser context and were lost on redirect, they are flashed now; the field guidance is rendered again; the update form is kept until the payment is captured, and its amount rule is declared the way the current form component expects, every submission failed on it before.
